# A Formal Theory of Phonological Equivalence

[![DOI](https://zenodo.org/badge/DOI/10.5281/zenodo.22967347.svg)](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22967347) [![Static Badge](https://img.shields.io/badge/OSF-10.17605%2FOSF.IO%2F6ND8S-red)](https://osf.io/6nd8s) [![Static Badge](https://img.shields.io/badge/CAPES-88887.186643/2025_00-red)](https://www.gov.br/capes/pt-br)

This repository contains the public code and data accompanying my doctoral dissertation, which is hosted [here](https://phd.alexandrebarroso.com) as an interactive experience. It collects the exact calculations, the formal proofs, the worked examples and the reduced empirical tables used in the dissertation, with a source-label index connecting the dissertation to its proof modules, exact computations and independent symbolic reconstructions. The integrated theory combines a first-order grammatical construction with a second-order account of what comparisons preserve and identify. The formal aspects of this research, and consequently this public-facing repo, were developed between 2024 and 2026. Some degree of chaos is expected, but everything is thoroughly checked even if somewhat unorganized. These files were developed in my private Macbook Air and Linux Ubuntu laptops, so scripts might need some fine tuning on their absolute paths to run. I did my best to make this whole repo machine-readable and parsing-friendly for automated checking (a sign of the times, I suppose). See also [PhonoScript](https://github.com/alexandre-barroso/phonoscript_project), a continuing research development whose reusable comparison operations are described in Appendix L.

The dissertation remains the source for theorem statements, proofs in prose, linguistic interpretation, empirical premises and scope limitations. These files supply the corresponding calculations, proofs and data. `results/` is a Python-generated machine-readable index for parsing purposes.

## The four themes

This repo is divided by themes, not by chapter structure. Mostly because this is how the research was developed before the dissertation was written, and I have decided to keep it as is for scientific transparency, and a touch of laziness due to the ballooning amount of files.

**Equivalence (chapters 2, 5–8; appendices B, G–M).** `python/phonological_equivalence/` implements the finite contract and its verdicts, the finite representation results, the MaxEnt algebra over exact rationals and integer polynomials, the continuous-HG profiles, the contextual and flux models and the application models; `python/verification/factcheck_*.py` recompute the printed comparison and evaluation results from the data tables. `wolfram/phonological_equivalence/` derives the MaxEnt results symbolically (`MaxEntG1G5.wl`, `MaxEntG6G9.wl`) and checks the Basic Syllable decomposition and the contact–phase capacity. `lean/phonological_equivalence` (library `PhonologicalCalculus`, 169 source modules including its root and audit, on Mathlib) proves the finite decision theorem, the recovery and factorization theorems, the MaxEnt carriers, realizations, vacuity, identification and capacity, the continuous-HG theorems including the heterogeneous-site theorem and its sharp obstruction, the maximum temperature of a productive grammar, the flux, support and selection results, and the application-level classifications.

**Opacity (chapters 3–4, appendix E).** `python/phonological_opacity/` and `wolfram/phonological_opacity/` evaluate the two fragments from their declarative specifications; `results/opacity/` holds the Lean exports they are compared with. `lean/phonological_opacity` (library `PhonologicalOpacity`, 1,252 source modules including its root and audit, on Mathlib) decides each Gua selection in the kernel on blocks of 169 candidates (`Gua/Blocks`, `Gua/Select`), the deletion controls and their joint incompatibility (`Gua/Deletion`), the attenuation interval at the declared weights (`Attenuation`), the exact free-weight focal projection (`Attenuation/FreeWeights`) and the Lithuanian region (`Lithuanian`). The free-weight theorem quantifies over real nonnegative weights shared by all eight products and requires every minimizer to have a documented surface. It proves feasibility exactly for `0 ≤ λ < 1/2`, for both reader variants, using one polynomial weight formula and two necessary competitor inequalities. `Attenuation/Productive/Baseline` and `Attenuation/Productive/Subject` separately prove the same exact attenuation projection when all reference vowels and glides of the eight inputs vary over the thirteen-value alphabet. The full domain contains 15,967,796 candidates, with the complete correct observation fibers preserved. A verified prefix bound and finite kernel decisions establish the shared parametric witness for every real attenuation in the interval; the two necessary competitor inequalities exclude the endpoint and all larger values. Equality of attenuation projections does not establish equality of the full nine-weight regions. The theorem keeps origins, order and prosodic domains fixed; it does not cover arbitrary new inputs, insertion of new origins, fusion, fission, reordering or probability normalization. The interaction library (`Attenuation/Typology`) proves the complete real-weight regions of 21 finite schemas, all 300 mode assignments, and both retention indexings. It checks complete candidate products and observation fibers before reducing the 600 assignments to 47 inequality systems. `Attenuation/Lardil.lean` separately reconstructs the three source forms on all 320 deletion candidates: its exact projection is `0 < λ < 1/2` precisely when apocope does not carry its left context. `MutualCounterfeeding`, `SharingObstruction`, and `InteractionObstruction` show that restricting attenuation alone while retaining a focal Gua or Lardil grammar still permits a mutual-counterfeeding grammar with separately refitted weights. `LardilCycle` records the positive-control cost of potential acyclicity; `SerialComparison` checks the two global orders and four directional blocks against the nine-form paradigm. These are results about the declared fragments and rival schedules. The schematic inventory adapts the contrasts in Frischoff and Rasin (2026, pp. 9–17, 28–29, 35–42;). Its descriptive labels identify constructed controls; they do not certify attestation or universal absence. The cells, the modes, the gauge, the labels and the exclusions of chapter 3 that hold for every weight and attenuation are proved in `lean/phonological_requirements` (`Cells.lean`, `Modes.lean`, `Contribution.lean`, `Transient.lean`).

The `productive_region_appendix` and `productive_region_subject` Python runners enumerate the full enlarged domains independently, retain exact coefficient/correctness classes with multiplicities and complete fibers, and check the affine witness margins. Their Wolfram counterparts reconstruct every class representative from the released Gua specification, check the observation map and multiplicity total, and verify the universal sign and endpoint identities. Run both Python modules before the corresponding `.wls` files; each writes its own certificate under the existing `results/requirements` layout.

**Grounding (chapters 2–4 and 7; appendices C, D and K).** `python/phonological_grounding/` computes the licensed queries and canonical contracts, the declaration language's adequacy, records, resolver variants, regions and winner locality (exports in `results/declaration_language/`), and the prediction battery of appendix K; `wolfram/phonological_grounding/` reconstructs the Lithuanian region, the battery and the declaration-language checks from the specifications alone. `lean/phonological_grounding` (library `PhonologicalGrounding`, 24 source modules on Mathlib) proves licensing and canonicity, discovery and the price of adaptation, strong-Kleene definedness with the zero-contribution and record theorems, footprint congruence, the frame-futility and unarisation results, the cycle theorem on carried cycles, and the shared-activity threshold with the non-robustness of the length battery.

**Requirements (chapters 3–8; appendices D–F and J–M).** `python/phonological_requirements/` is the reference interpreter over typed declarations (structures, admissibility, the productive generator with its finite reduction, readers, the retained construction's evaluation, footprints, the interaction graph, the alignment automaton), the source-native fragments kept apart from the core (`frag_*.py`), the phenomenon-specific runners that write the certificates, and the four introductory and boundary examples (`examples/`). `wolfram/phonological_requirements/` derives the parameter regions of the transient-environment, accent, morphological-accent and interaction-typology results by quantifier elimination and checks the modes, the feeding chain, the covering counts, the created-position quotient and maximum temperature, the schwa law and the heterogeneous sites. `lean/phonological_requirements` (library `PhonologicalRequirements`, 29 source modules, Lean core only) proves definedness, discharge and persistence, the excluded family and the Lithuanian exclusion, the footprint's soundness and the narrow footprint's unsoundness, noninterference, cycles, the cells, modes and gauge, the inert cell and transient environments with the Arapaho verdict, indexation collapse, length-bounded feeding, directional deletion as a covering with its tie counts, majority against weighting, the created-position quotient, bounded variation under splicing, the schwa law, the first-step barrier, admissibility with its preservation and equivariance, the static discipline, and the registered specialization of the 527 declarations of the 109 fragments to the one core (`Registered.lean`, generated by `registered_export.py`).

The Russian inventory-gap dossier distinguishes Gouskova’s lexical restrictions from the underspecified alternative in `gapped_inventory.py`. `gapped_scope.py` adds a fully specified lexical-admission control and independently checks all binary candidates for the 46 registered inputs. Both encodings reproduce those observations at attenuation 1/8 and 1; the finite forms do not identify attenuation. The richer markedness encoding is also checked with refitted weights on this same observed domain; those fits do not establish its behavior on inadmissible source inputs. The source is Gouskova (2023), “MSCs in Positional Neutralisation: The Problem of Gapped Inventories,” manuscript §§2.3–3.2 and 4. The local 45-page manuscript’s printed pagination is retained separately from the journal pagination.

`Application/GappedScope.lean` proves the boundary of a fixed finite-weight comparison on constructed /tⁿba/ inputs: the fully voiced candidate costs n(I+λV)+V, while the fully voiceless competitor costs I+P. Strict voiceless selection on final /t/ requires I+λV>0, so some length defeats full voicing under any one finite weight vector. The theorem concerns the specified voicing-only candidate family, not attested arbitrarily long Russian clusters. Python checks the native coefficients on the finite source cases and long constructed pairs; Wolfram independently reconstructs the finite calculations and the decisive inequality. Source manuscript (18) prints six profiles although its prose says seven; all 24 strict rankings yield those six profiles on its four displayed inputs. No complete source-theory translation or productive native-interpreter correspondence follows from finite reproduction.

Run `PYTHONPATH=python python3 -m phonological_requirements.gapped_scope`, then `WolframKernel -noinit -noprompt -script wolfram/phonological_requirements/gapped_scope.wls`. The outputs are `results/requirements/certificates/gapped_scope.json` and `results/requirements/wolfram/gapped_scope.json`.

The syllable-contact fragment (`contact_scale.py`, `frag_contact.py`) uses weighted cumulative sonority thresholds on declared candidate sets. Its source is Gouskova (2004), “Relational hierarchies in Optimality Theory: the case of syllable contact,” especially pp. 208–211, 218–238 and 240–244. The source ranks distance-stratum constraints; the weighted reconstruction is a separate analysis. It normalizes vowel-harmony and transcription details, fixes the relevant syllable domains, and represents gemination by a long consonant plus an absent suffix origin. These choices do not reconstruct the source's full correspondence or moraic theory. Its order-preservation reader checks the reference order of adjacent surviving correspondents. On the registered candidates, which allow at most one adjacent exchange, this counts all reversed surviving pairs; that equality is not asserted for unrestricted reordering.

The Gran Canarian apocope reconstruction follows the deletion-only, stress-preserving domain in Broś and Nazarov (2023, pp. 17, 27). Its MaxEnt calculations sum every correspondence producing an observed surface. Table 6 supplies rounded percentage weights repeated across four schematic word pairs, not independent lexical token counts. Retention yields the bounded fed-counterfeeding example; matching the latent frequency contrast additionally uses input-context faithfulness. The source's contextual-faithfulness and variable-rule alternatives remain substantive comparisons (pp. 35, 40). Numerical fits do not certify a global maximum, identify attenuation, or establish a unique mechanism. Zero-count bounds are explicitly conditional iid benchmarks, not inferences from unmodeled clustered speech data.

The Arapaho accent fragment (`accent_encoding.py`, `frag_accent.py`) adapts Bogomolets (2023, pp. 231–242; local uncorrected proof) and the clash contrasts in Cowell and Moss (2008, pp. 28–29). Its 603 candidates preserve segments and syllables, vary accent presence and association, and allow at most one new accent. Preaccenting locative morphology uses the preceding morpheme's final nucleus; the segment-tier successor test makes that restriction explicit. The stress patterns are normalized projections, not complete literal transcriptions or a reconstruction of the source's tone and phonetic-stress analysis. A refitted current-markedness comparison with strictly positive weights selects all ten registered patterns other than `PUTDOWN_3PL`; the certificate preserves that failure and the retained analysis's complete winner sets. These finite observations therefore do not establish that retention is necessary. Formula-level region proofs and correspondence between those formulas and the native declarations are separate verification obligations.

The morphological-accent fragment adapts 91 accent patterns from Alderete (2001), *Morphologically Governed Accent in Optimality Theory*, Chapters 2, 3 and 5. Its 3,674 candidates preserve segments and permit at most one new accent; normalized forms are accent projections rather than literal source transcriptions. The source's output-to-output anti-faithfulness and metrical representations are not fully translated by the fixed-reference declarations. In particular, per-accent deletion requirements and directly specified landing sites differ from wide-scope anti-faithfulness and independently ranked alignment. All 91 registered patterns also admit exact fitting weights at the current-markedness endpoint, with the same declaration inventory and ordinary reference faithfulness; these observations do not identify retention. Positive numerical searches are accepted only after exact rational checking of the complete observed fibers. Failed branches remain unresolved. The recursive reset comparison requires a unique observable base winner representable with at most one accent per morpheme and rejects ambiguous cases.

The Nuer fragment projects 28 tonal contexts from Monich (2020), *Nuer Tonal Inventory*, into a fixed-syllable generator with tonal deletion, insertion, retuning, permutation and fusion. It reproduces the registered sets at the displayed weights; this does not translate the source's complete autosegmental analysis or predict frequencies of optional variants. The registry omits the nonfinal HL–LH alternative explicitly discussed on source page 35, and its isolated nonfinal rising-tone item abstracts away from the following high suffix in Figure 9. Positive fitting witnesses are checked with exact rational arithmetic against complete observed fibers. Failed branch searches remain unresolved, including the simultaneous exact-tie interpretation of variation. The dynamic-programming comparisons concern the declared pruned option sets; they do not establish pruning for arbitrary phrase lengths. Direct current-state markedness must be evaluated separately: setting attenuation to one still retains pressure from a former final-tone subject in `FINAL_LOW`.

The cophonology runner separates arithmetic reconstruction of twelve printed tableaux from typed Moro and Dogon fragments adapted from Sande, Jenks and Inkelas (2020), *Cophonologies by Ph(r)ase*. The source's syntactic phases, vocabulary insertion, weight adjustments and cyclic evaluation are not translated in full. Moro uses supplied aspect and domain labels; its tone generator enumerates each resulting structure once when computing probabilities. Dogon permits a restricted set of melody overlays. Its docking readers inspect associations, with realization compatibility imposed by that generator. The checked Dogon scores are independent of attenuation, and the selected Moro winners also admit a common current-state refit. The Nanga proportions in the source are illustrative values adopted without frequency data, not measured corpus frequencies. Reproducing their rounded split does not establish empirical identification or equality of complete probability laws.

The correspondence runner (`agreement_by_correspondence.py`, `frag_abc.py`) is a schematic three-segment example inspired by Yang (2026), *Reduplicative identity driven by surface correspondence*. It demonstrates alternative repairs and the loss of pressure when a derived correspondence partner is absent. It does not implement the Huozhou mora-affix analysis, reduplicative fission or the full source constraint inventory. In particular, its successive rounded-vowel search differs from the source’s all-pair correspondence requirement, and its single-partner identity reader differs from a filtered correspondence chain. The displayed weight comparisons are finite examples, not universal ranking-necessity or repair-independence results. On its fixed reference, positive attenuation can be absorbed into the free correspondence weight; the example therefore does not identify attenuation.

The Nez Perce reconstruction (`stratal_syncope.py`, `frag_stratal.py`) adapts 33 contexts from Kiparsky (2021), *Phonology to the rescue: Nez Perce morphology revisited*. Its finite generator and observation map project the source's allomorphy, shortening, syncope and accent contrasts; vowel harmony, secondary stress and the complete fusion analysis remain outside that projection. Fitting checks use the full finite candidate sets and retain all winning outputs. Hard-constraint pruning is accepted only after checking actual scores, and failed numerical branch searches remain unresolved. The 33 registered outcome sets also admit a shared, strictly positive 29-weight vector under direct current-state aggregation of the same readers. Those readers still use reference length, lexical accent and stratum information, so this comparison establishes neither a wholly surface-based analysis nor equivalence to the full source grammar. It does show that these projected outcomes alone do not identify retention.

The Tonkawa runner (`reduplication_template.py`, `frag_redup.py`) adapts Gouskova (2007), *The reduplicative template in Tonkawa*, on 30 registered forms and two separately reported exceptions. The source's incomplete stress records limit the status of its foot patterns. The declared generator restricts copying, deletion and syllabification; it does not reconstruct unrestricted source GEN. The two successful completion variants add foot markedness and modify the treatment of lexically conditioned syncope. Both variants also fit the 30 forms with current-state evaluation, and refitting can preserve the registered outcomes after removing some reduplicant-specific requirements. These are bounded expressibility and component-dependence checks, not proof that every component is independently necessary or that the source analysis has been translated in full. Exact positive witnesses are checked against all admitted candidate winners; unresolved branch searches and the two exceptional forms remain explicit. The extended finite generator also admits the constructed truncating pattern /pataka/ → [pataka], /RED-pataka/ → [papa] at separately fitted weights in both completion variants. This is a counterexample to exclusion by that declared family, not an attested-language result or a joint fit to the Tonkawa forms and the hypothetical inputs.

The introductory comparisons are checked by `Finite/OrderComparison.lean`, `Gua/Opening.lean`, `factcheck_opening_comparisons.py` and `OpeningComparisons.wls`. The Gua rows are derived from the native readers. The neutral OT example preserves the winner while reversing one candidate pair; the full directed relation has two changed entries. The finite examples also distinguish order preservation from probability preservation. These checks concern the displayed examples and typed transport, not an independent empirical result.

## Reproduction

### Python

The `phonological_equivalence` package uses the standard library only (Python 3.10 or later). The `phonological_requirements` package, the `phonological_grounding` predictions and the stress suites use Python 3.12 with `numpy`, `scipy` and `sympy` for the fitted and symbolic parts; every exact part uses `fractions` and integers. Run everything from the repository root.

```sh
python3 python/verification/coverage.py
python3 python/phonological_opacity/reproduce.py --engine python
python3 python/phonological_grounding/reproduce.py --engine python
python3 python/verification/audit_requirements.py --phase reproduction
python3 python/verification/audit_requirements.py --phase runners --shard 1/3
python3 python/verification/audit_requirements.py --phase runners --shard 2/3
python3 python/verification/audit_requirements.py --phase runners --shard 3/3
python3 python/verification/audit_requirements.py --phase examples
python3 python/verification/audit_requirements.py --phase wolfram
python3 python/verification/audit_requirements.py --phase stress
python3 python/verification/audit_requirements.py --phase lean
```

`audit_requirements.py` without arguments runs every phase in one process and writes `results/requirements/audit.json`; a phase run writes `results/requirements/audit_<phase>.json` (with the shard in the name). The phases are: the two reproduction entry points and the bridge and leakage fact-checks; the runners, which re-derive every certificate in `results/requirements/certificates/` (sharded so that each shard stays well under an hour on a laptop; the enlarged-domain checks dominate the total runtime); the four examples, the certificate inventory and the byte-identical regeneration of `Registered.lean`; the byte-identical regeneration of the emitted Wolfram scripts and the execution of every Wolfram script; the six stress suites (`stress_*.py`, seeded, thousands of random instances each); and the Lean build with its axiom report. A runner is invoked directly as `python3 -m phonological_requirements.<name>` from `python/`; the example scripts as `python3 python/phonological_requirements/examples/<name>.py`.

### Uyghur joint raising and harmony

Chapter 8 and Appendix M test a declared four-state feature fragment against analytical records derived from Connor Mayer's Uyghur corpus study (`mayer2025largeScaleCorpusOpacityUyghur`). The records are written-corpus article–spelling incidences after within-article collapse. The released tables contain isolated roots, features and aggregate counts. They contain no newspaper prose or article identities; the supplied analysis repository's GPL-3.0 license is retained in `data/uyghur/COPYING`. The parser analysis and the grammatical reference representation have distinct roles, specified in the dissertation and `data/uyghur/specification.json`.

The comparison uses 524 eligible roots, of which 515 contribute 58,577 covered target incidences. The frozen fits include all six model families, both lexical-information protocols, three sample/offset variants, two beta hyperpriors, both Gaussian prior scales for Harmonic Uniformity, and both Laplace and 80-node quadrature approximations where applicable. `results/uyghur/` preserves every selected-regularization and zero-penalty comparison. The zero-penalty numerical candidates do not by themselves certify existence of a finite unregularized maximum-likelihood estimate.

```sh
python3 python/phonological_requirements/uyghur/experiment.py --validate-only
python3 python/phonological_requirements/uyghur/reproduce.py --likelihood-checks
```

The second command verifies the frozen files, independently reconstructs the nested root partitions, scores every prediction from the primitive requirements, checks access to lexical information, and reproduces all 384 model-score rows and 4,320 paired contrasts. It also reports calibration and measured numerical-integration discrepancies. Its paired-root bootstrap uses 5,000 resamples with seed 2026092007, conditional on the fitted models and folds; it does not include refitting uncertainty or establish independence of speakers or articles. Outputs go to `build/uyghur/replay/`, which must be a new directory; use `--output-dir` for another replay.

New fitting is a separate operation:

```sh
python3 python/phonological_requirements/uyghur/experiment.py --fit --rho-prior uniform --hu-tau 20 --output-dir build/uyghur/new_uniform20
```

The other declared settings use `--rho-prior beta_half_half` and/or `--hu-tau 50`. Fitting enforces the same convergence, information and input-integrity checks as the released analysis. The public specification replaces private administrative records, so newly generated cache identifiers differ from the preserved originals; the numerical model is unchanged.

The linguistic comparison has two connected results. A retained harmonic requirement constrains raising, allowing the joint response to distinguish grammars that agree on conditional harmony; a specified lexical adjustment can remove that distinction. Under the principal root-balanced prediction criterion, stronger rivals outperform the retention model. The result locates the information and grammatical commitments responsible for discrimination and does not establish general predictive superiority for retention. The Lean modules `Application/UyghurJoint.lean` and `Application/UyghurPredictions.lean`, the independent Python scorer, and `wolfram/phonological_requirements/uyghur_joint.wls` expose complementary parts of that argument.

### Wolfram Language

Scripts run with the kernel, for example

```sh
/Applications/Wolfram.app/Contents/MacOS/WolframKernel -noinit -noprompt -script wolfram/phonological_requirements/modes_check.wls
```

or with `wolframscript -file`. The requirements scripts write their results to `results/requirements/wolfram/`; the grounding and opacity scripts are driven by the two `reproduce.py` entry points with `--engine wolfram`; the two equivalence packages are loaded and queried, as in

```sh
wolframscript -code 'Get["wolfram/phonological_equivalence/MaxEntG1G5.wl"]; Print[MaxEntG1G5Calculations`MaxEntG1G5Results[]]'
```

and the equivalence fact-check scripts print their verdicts. `MaxEntPackagesCheck.wls` checks the two packages’ exact returned values, identities and declared finite ranges. A region script bounds quantifier elimination with `TimeConstrained`. The audit rejects missing or stale output files, unresolved symbolic expressions and `TIMEOUT` results. A calculation at finitely many parameter values establishes only those instances. ## Conventions and maintenance protocol

The repository is code and data only, and future changes keep it so.

1. `lean/`, `python/` and `wolfram/` each contain exactly the four theme folders; a new artifact goes into the theme of the chapter it serves, under a file name that names its content (a phenomenon, a construction, a theorem's subject), never a chapter number, a section number or an internal identifier.
2. This README and the data indexes supply the narrative documentation and source links. Lean, Python and Wolfram source files contain code without prose comments or docstrings. Register Lean module labels and ledger codes in `data/dissertation/lean_source_labels.json`; `coverage.py` resolves them alongside its explicit artifact mappings. 3. `results/` holds machine-readable certificates, fitted values and comparison tables. Regenerated scripts and exports go to untracked `build/` directories. Compare the declared numerical or exact fields, rather than treating volatile timings or configuration-derived identifiers as scientific results.
4. Expected outputs live in the test harness, never in a grammar: the leakage fact-check reruns generation and evaluation with the observation files unreadable, and the source-name test erases every language and example name from the fields the evaluator can reach.
5. A new dissertation statement is registered in `data/dissertation/statements.tsv` with its current locator and in `coverage.py` with its artifacts; a new Lean module's statement labels are registered in `data/dissertation/lean_source_labels.json`; a new runner is added to `RUNNERS` in `audit_requirements.py` and writes its certificate through `certificate.write`; a new Wolfram script is added to `WOLFRAM_SCRIPTS`. 6. Lean sources never use `sorry`, `admit`, `native_decide` or axiom declarations; a finite computation is decided with `decide +kernel` on a piece small enough to check in seconds, and a heavy library is built one module at a time.
7. Use explicit bounds, fixed seeds, convergence checks and capped symbolic queries where applicable. The exhaustive generators and clean proof builds can take substantial time; use the documented shards and serial opacity-module builds to control memory.
